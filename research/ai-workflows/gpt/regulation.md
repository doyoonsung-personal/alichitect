**Research date: 2 November 2026.** This is a deployment-screening report for the catalog and proposal generator, not legal advice. “Design implication” below means an engineering recommendation derived from the cited rule; it is not necessarily a statutory requirement.

The app should store **binding law, supervisory expectations, voluntary guidance, and unconfirmed requirements as separate categories**. It should also separate the customer’s role as an AI deployer from its possible role as a system or model provider.

I found no contradiction to the supplied Alibaba Cloud baseline in this regulatory research. Those product facts were not independently re-audited here.

**1. European Union: AI Act**

The AI Act classifies systems principally by intended use and operator role. Singapore hosting does not remove its potential application: it covers providers placing systems or models on the EU market and, in specified circumstances, providers and deployers outside the EU whose outputs are used in the EU. [Article 2](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-2) **The high-risk deadlines have actually been delayed**, rather than merely proposed for delay: the Commission reports that the AI Omnibus entered into force on **27 July 2026**, moving Annex III requirements to **2 December 2027** and regulated-product high-risk requirements to **2 August 2028**. [Commission announcement](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/ai-omnibus-enters-force)

| Requirement | Position on 2 November 2026 | Design implication |
|---|---|---|
| Prohibited practices and AI literacy | Prohibitions and AI-literacy provisions have applied since **2 February 2025**. [Commission FAQ](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/navigating-ai-act) | Screen intended uses before drawing a deployable solution; record operator training and ownership. |
| Chatbot disclosure | Article 50 applies from **2 August 2026**. Providers must ensure people know they are interacting directly with AI, unless that is obvious in context. [Timeline](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/eu-ai-act-implementation-timeline?trk=public_post_comment-text), [Article 50(1)](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-50) | Add an explicit disclosure at the first interaction, including email conversations where the AI identity would otherwise be unclear. |
| Synthetic-content marking | Providers of systems generating synthetic text, audio, images or video have machine-readable marking obligations, subject to technical feasibility and specified exceptions. This differs from a visible “AI-generated” label. [Article 50(2)](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-50) | Track visible disclosure and machine-readable provenance separately; test whether export or email tools strip metadata. |
| Existing synthetic-content systems | Systems placed on the market before **2 August 2026** have a transition until **2 December 2026** for Article 50(2). This is not a general postponement of chatbot disclosure. [Article 111(4)](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-111) | Store market-entry date and the particular obligation receiving transitional treatment. |
| Deepfakes and public-interest text | Deployers have disclosure duties for deepfakes and certain AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest; exceptions include specified human editorial review arrangements. [Article 50(4)](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-50) | Do not turn this into a claim that every internally drafted document legally needs a visible label. |
| Recruiting and credit | Annex III includes recruitment/application filtering, candidate evaluation, and assessment of natural persons’ creditworthiness or credit scores; financial-fraud detection is excepted from that credit category. [Annex III, 4 and 5(b)](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/annex-3) | Flag candidate ranking and loan decisions for high-risk classification review. An ordinary support bot should not inherit this classification merely because a bank uses it. |
| High-risk deployer controls | When applicable, Article 26 requires use according to instructions, competent human oversight, monitoring, incident handling and retention of controlled automatically generated logs for at least **six months**, subject to other applicable law. [Article 26](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-26) | Draw meaningful review/override routes and versioned audit records. Do not impose six-month retention on every chatbot. |
| Fundamental-rights impact assessment | Article 27 applies to specified deployers, including certain public-service deployments and creditworthiness and life/health-insurance use cases. It is not an assessment required of every agent. [Article 27](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-27) | Add a conditional assessment requirement alongside, rather than replacing, the privacy impact assessment. |

**GPAI obligations need a separate catalog rule.** Model-provider duties include technical documentation, information for downstream system providers, a copyright-compliance policy and a public training-content summary. Merely calling a model API does not by itself make the customer the provider of that model. [Article 53](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-53) Systemic-risk GPAI providers have additional evaluation, risk-management, serious-incident and cybersecurity duties. [Article 55](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-55) GPAI rules began applying on **2 August 2025**, but providers of models placed on the market before that date have until **2 August 2027**. [Commission FAQ](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/navigating-ai-act), [Article 111(3)](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-111)

**GDPR remains a separate deployment gate.** Significant decisions based solely on automated personal-data processing engage Article 22; nominal human involvement is insufficient. The EDPB reproduces the established requirement that reviewers have competence and authority to change the decision. [EDPB discussion, paragraphs 22–23 and footnote 40; document is a consultation version](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2025-09/edpb_guidelines_202503_interplay-dsa-gdpr_v1_en.pdf) Processing likely to create high privacy risk requires a DPIA before processing begins. [Commission DPIA guidance](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/obligations_en?prefLang=ro)

**Timeline uncertainty:** official sources disagree on the national regulatory-sandbox deadline: the service desk lists August 2027 while the Council timeline reports December 2027. This does not change the corroborated customer high-risk deadlines above; avoid encoding the sandbox date without further legislative-text verification. [Service-desk timeline](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/eu-ai-act-implementation-timeline?trk=public_post_comment-text), [Council timeline](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/policies/artificial-intelligence-act/timeline-artificial-intelligence/)

**2. South Korea: AI Basic Act and PIPA**

The AI Basic Act and its Enforcement Decree took effect on **22 January 2026**. MSIT announced an enforcement grace period of **at least one year**; that announcement should not be represented as postponing the Act’s effective date. Its exact eventual enforcement endpoint is **unconfirmed** in this review. [MSIT implementation announcement](https://www.msit.go.kr/eng/bbs/view.do?bbsSeqNo=42&mId=4&nttSeqNo=1214&sCode=eng) The Act requires advance assessment of whether a system is high-impact and imposes additional safeguards on qualifying systems. Its statutory definition combines potential serious impact with specified areas, including healthcare, hiring and lending. [Current translated Act, Articles 2 and 33–35](https://elaw.klri.re.kr/kor_mobile/viewer.do?hseq=73499&key=54&type=sogan)

| Requirement | Confirmed position | Design implication |
|---|---|---|
| Advance AI notice | Users must be informed in advance when products/services use high-impact or generative AI. MSIT allows notices through interfaces, terms, contracts and appropriate offline methods. [MSIT transparency guidance](https://www.msit.go.kr/eng/bbs/view.do?bbsSeqNo=42&nttSeqNo=1215) | Add a Korean-market disclosure control independently of high-impact classification. |
| Generative-output labelling | Article 31 distinguishes advance notice from identification of generated content; MSIT guidance differentiates content remaining inside a service from exported/distributed content. [MSIT transparency guidance](https://www.msit.go.kr/eng/bbs/view.do?bbsSeqNo=42&nttSeqNo=1215) | Test generated emails, attachments and exported proposals, not just the chat screen. Exact marking treatment should be checked against the service’s content format. |
| High-impact safeguards | Article 34 requires risk management, technically feasible explanations, user protection, human oversight and retained evidence of safety/trustworthiness measures. [Act, Article 34](https://elaw.klri.re.kr/kor_mobile/viewer.do?hseq=73499&key=54&type=sogan) | Include responsible human roles, override/stop controls, assessment records and decision evidence. |
| Impact assessment | Article 35 uses a duty to **endeavour** to assess fundamental-rights impacts; public bodies should preferentially consider assessed products. Do not describe it as a universal mandatory certification. [Act, Article 35](https://elaw.klri.re.kr/kor_mobile/viewer.do?hseq=73499&key=54&type=sogan) | Record assessment status and procurement expectations separately from certification. |
| PIPA automated decisions | Article 37-2 provides refusal rights for fully automated decisions significantly affecting rights or obligations, explanation rights and transparency about decision criteria/procedures. Refusal has exceptions for consent, legal provisions and contract-related processing; explanation should not be treated as automatically extinguished by those exceptions. [Commission’s 2026 official review of PIPA, pp. 6–7](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=SWD%3A2026%3A231%3AFIN) | Provide an explanation/request-review route and record the basis for accepting or refusing a request. A model-generated explanation alone is not evidence of the actual decision process. |

PIPA’s automated-decision provisions began operating in **March 2024**, independently of the 2026 AI Act regime. The historically cited commencement is **15 March 2024**; the official law version is dated accordingly, although its article text did not render fully in this research interface. [Official PIPA version](https://law.go.kr/LSW/lsInfoP.do?ancYnChk=0&chrClsCd=010202&efYd=20240315&joNo=004100&lsiSeq=248613&urlMode=lsInfoP)

**3. Singapore: model governance, agentic guidance and PDPA**

Singapore’s Model AI Governance Framework is practical governance guidance, covering internal accountability, human involvement, operations and stakeholder communication. Its second edition dates from **2020**. [PDPC framework](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/help-and-resources/2020/01/second-edition-of-model-artificial-intelligence-governance-framework) IMDA introduced an agentic framework in **January 2026**; the official indexed page reports a **May 2026 update, version 1.5**, adding multi-agent, third-party-agent and automation-bias guidance. The live landing page was inaccessible, so the updated document’s detailed wording is **unconfirmed** here; the original official factsheet was accessible. [IMDA framework page](https://www.imda.gov.sg/AI), [Official agentic factsheet](https://www.imda.gov.sg/-/media/imda/files/news-and-events/media-room/media-releases/2026/01/factsheet-model-ai-governance-framework-for-agentic-ai.pdf)

| Instrument | Status and dates | Design implication |
|---|---|---|
| Agentic governance framework | Voluntary guidance. Government explicitly recommends human review before high-stakes or irreversible actions and identifying approval checkpoints/action boundaries. [MDDI, 6 May 2026](https://www.mddi.gov.sg/newsroom/mddi-s-response-to-pq-on-ensuring-meaningful-human-accountability-for-public-facing-autonomous-ai-agents-and-pathways-to-mandatory-governance-in-high-risk-sectors/) | Bound tool permissions, transaction sizes and autonomous actions; define a person accountable for each checkpoint. |
| Agent lifecycle controls | The original framework addresses risk boundaries, meaningful human accountability, lifecycle controls, and user transparency/training. [IMDA factsheet](https://www.imda.gov.sg/-/media/imda/files/news-and-events/media-room/media-releases/2026/01/factsheet-model-ai-governance-framework-for-agentic-ai.pdf) | Add tool allowlists, interruption paths, testing and monitoring. Label these as recommended controls unless another rule makes them obligatory. |
| Chatbot transparency guidance | Government confirmed on **8 September 2026** that its chatbot transparency guidelines establish a **voluntary** baseline for explaining capabilities, limitations and safeguards. [MDDI response](https://www.mddi.gov.sg/newsroom/mddi-response-to-pq-on-mandating-baseline-transparency-disclosures-for-sensitive-applications-of-ai-in-healthcare-children-and-personal-data/) | Show capabilities and limits, plus a human-contact route; do not call this a universal statutory chatbot-labelling mandate. |
| PDPA and AI | PDPA applies to personal-data use in AI development, testing, monitoring and deployment. The **1 March 2024** advisory guidelines explain consent, conditional exceptions, accountability and data-intermediary responsibilities; the guidelines themselves are nonbinding. [PDPC AI guidelines, §§2, 4–11](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/files/pdpc/pdf-files/advisory-guidelines/advisory-guidelines-on-the-use-of-personal-data-in-ai-recommendation-and-decision-systems.pdf) | Record purpose and processing basis; minimise prompt data; separate inference from training reuse; define vendor protection and retention responsibilities. |

**Verification gap:** reports identify final PDPC generative-AI guidelines dated **20 July 2026**, but the official landing page and document returned access challenges. Treat that date and the final guideline-specific requirements as **unconfirmed from accessible primary text**, rather than silently using the June consultation as final law. [Official landing page attempted](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/organisations/regulations-decisions/regulatory-guidance/advisory-guidelines-on-use-of-personal-data-in-generative-ai), [Official document attempted](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/assets/143cb9d4-532e-4cca-9a77-bcc0415ca294)

**4. Japan: AI Promotion Act and business guidelines**

Japan’s Act on Promotion of Research and Development, and Utilization of AI-related Technology was promulgated and partially commenced on **4 June 2025**, with full commencement on **1 September 2025**. [Cabinet Office](https://www8.cao.go.jp/cstp/ai/ai_act/ai_act.html) Its structure promotes AI development and use, establishes government coordination and supports investigation and guidance concerning inappropriate uses; existing laws remain relevant. [Official Act overview](https://www8.cao.go.jp/cstp/ai/ai_hou_gaiyou_en.pdf) The current business-guideline edition found is **version 1.2, 31 March 2026**. Its implementation material addresses governance across developers, providers and business users. [METI/MIC official appendix overview](https://www.meti.go.jp/shingikai/mono_info_service/ai_shakai_jisso/pdf/20260331_15.pdf)

| Proposal issue | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Legal status | Describe the Promotion Act as legislation and the business guidelines as governance guidance. Do not infer an EU-style high-risk conformity-assessment regime from the Act’s title. The official overview describes promotion, policy coordination, investigation and guidance. [Cabinet Office overview](https://www8.cao.go.jp/cstp/ai/ai_hou_gaiyou_en.pdf) |
| Human oversight and logs | **Design implication:** assign an accountable business owner, document intended use and model/tool changes, provide escalation, and retain risk/testing evidence. These operationalise the guideline’s governance cycle. [METI/MIC v1.2 implementation overview](https://www.meti.go.jp/shingikai/mono_info_service/ai_shakai_jisso/pdf/20260331_15.pdf) |
| Disclosure | **Design implication:** explain AI use and limitations where material to the user’s decision. A blanket Japanese statutory requirement to label every generative output was **not confirmed** by the sources reviewed. |
| Data location | Evaluate APPI foreign-transfer requirements separately; the Promotion Act is not evidence that Singapore processing is automatically permitted. [PPC foreign-transfer guidelines](https://www.ppc.go.jp/personalinfo/legal/guidelines_offshore/) |

The main v1.2 English PDF returned 403; the official appendix overview was readable. Any proposal depending on a particular sentence in the main text needs that sentence verified before being encoded as a requirement.

**5. Singapore hosting: residency and international transfers**

**Residency and lawful transfer are different checks.** EU, Korean, Japanese and Singapore rules provide conditional transfer mechanisms; selecting a Singapore region is not itself one of those mechanisms. [EU transfer safeguards](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX%3A32021D0914), [Korean transfer rules](https://www.law.go.kr/lsInfoP.do?lsiSeq=270351&urlMode=engLsInfoR&viewCls=engLsInfoR), [Japan PPC](https://www.ppc.go.jp/personalinfo/legal/guidelines_offshore/), [Singapore PDPC decision](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/files/pdpc/pdf-files/commissions-decisions/decision---nui-and-newriis--23062021.pdf)

| Origin / regime | What matters for Singapore processing | Proposal and diagram rule |
|---|---|---|
| EU/EEA | Singapore is absent from the Commission’s adequacy list. A suitable Chapter V mechanism is therefore needed where the operation constitutes a restricted transfer; SCCs are a common route. SCCs also require assessment of destination-country laws/practices and relevant safeguards. [Adequacy list](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/international-dimension-data-protection/adequacy-decisions_en), [2021 SCCs, especially clause 14](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX%3A32021D0914) | Record exporter/importer roles, applicable contractual module, transfer assessment, subprocessors and onward transfers. Do not suggest that EU adequacy for Japan or Korea extends to Singapore. |
| Korea | PIPA Article 28-8 covers overseas provision—including inquiry/access—entrusted processing and storage. Routes include separate transfer consent and, under conditions, entrusted processing/storage necessary for a contract with the person plus disclosure or notification. [PIPA Article 28-8](https://www.law.go.kr/lsInfoP.do?lsiSeq=270351&urlMode=engLsInfoR&viewCls=engLsInfoR) | Record destination, recipient, purpose, data categories, timing/method, retention and applicable transfer basis. A generic “cloud consent” flag is insufficient. |
| Japan | APPI Article 28 generally requires advance consent for provision to a foreign third party unless an applicable exception or equivalent-protection arrangement applies. Consent-related information and ongoing verification duties depend on the route used; enhanced requirements date from **1 April 2022**. [PPC guidelines, §§2, 4 and 6](https://www.ppc.go.jp/personalinfo/legal/guidelines_offshore/) | Record the selected route and recipient safeguards. Do not assume ordinary domestic outsourcing treatment settles foreign processing. |
| Singapore and onward transfers | The transfer-limitation obligation requires comparable protection through applicable mechanisms; contractual obligations and binding corporate rules are recognised tools. [PDPC decision, paragraphs 4–7](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/files/pdpc/pdf-files/commissions-decisions/decision---nui-and-newriis--23062021.pdf) | Include destinations of tools, logs, backups and support access. A Singapore cloud supplier can still involve overseas transfers. [IMDA implementation guide, §7.7.4](https://www.imda.gov.sg/-/media/imda/files/programme/dptm/dptm-ss-implementation-guide.pdf) |
| Philippines | Outsourcing contracts must specify processing geography and require documented instructions, confidentiality, security and controls over subprocessors. The controller remains responsible for the arrangement. [NPC implementing rules, §§43–45](https://privacy.gov.ph/implementing-rules-regulations-data-privacy-act-2012/) | Require a documented processor chain, processing locations, deletion/return arrangements and audit support. These are existing privacy obligations, not a new 2026 AI-specific regime. |
| Malaysia | The regulator publishes country-specific cross-border-transfer guidelines. The primary PDF could not be extracted in this review; its detailed conditions and commencement treatment are **unconfirmed here**. [JPDP official guidelines](https://www.pdp.gov.my/en/akta/personal-data-protection-guidelines-on-cross-border-transfer-of-personal-data-cbpdt/) | Trigger Malaysia-specific transfer review rather than copying Singapore’s rule. |
| ASEAN generally | ASEAN model contractual clauses are voluntary templates, endorsed in **January 2021**, intended to support applicable national requirements. They do not create a single ASEAN permission to transfer data. [ASEAN MCCs](https://asean.org/wp-content/uploads/3-ASEAN-Model-Contractual-Clauses-for-Cross-Border-Data-Flows_Final.pdf) | Keep country-specific checks. Detailed current requirements for Thailand, Vietnam, Indonesia and other Southeast Asian jurisdictions are **unconfirmed in this report**. |

**Recommended drawing rule:** show location and recipient separately for the mailbox, ingestion service, runtime, model inference, retrieval store, tool endpoints, telemetry, backups and human support access. Mark unknown destinations explicitly. Apply this to prompt content, retrieved passages, attachments and traces—not just the primary database.

For the supplied Alibaba baseline, a Singapore workflow connected to Hong Kong observability should therefore show a distinct Singapore → Hong Kong telemetry boundary. Whether it carries personal data depends on trace configuration. This is an architectural implication of the supplied product facts, not a newly verified product claim.

**6. Sector overlays**

Financial services and healthcare need additional screening because sector expectations can apply even where the general AI framework is voluntary or a high-risk AI deadline is deferred. MAS now has final AI-risk guidance, while Singapore health authorities expressly confirm that patient-data security requirements apply to third-party cloud AI. [MAS final guidance](https://www.mas.gov.sg/regulation/guidelines/guidelines-on-artificial-intelligence-risk-management-for-financial-institutions), [MOH cloud-AI statement](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/data-security-requirements-apply-to-ai-tools-that-process-patient-data/)

| Sector | Confirmed rule / date | Workflow implications |
|---|---|---|
| Singapore financial services | MAS published final AI Risk Management Guidelines on **7 October 2026**, covering all FIs and AI technologies proportionately. They take effect **7 October 2027**; sections 3–4 apply then, with sections 5–6 by **7 October 2028**. [Guidelines](https://www.mas.gov.sg/regulation/guidelines/guidelines-on-artificial-intelligence-risk-management-for-financial-institutions), [MAS announcement](https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/media-releases/2026/mas-sets-out-supervisory-expectations-on-responsible-ai-adoption-by-financial-institutions?trk=public_post_comment-text) | Inventory models and agent use cases; assign board/management accountability; assess materiality; test, monitor and control changes; obtain third-party assurance and plan service suspension/replacement. These are supervisory expectations, not a standalone AI licensing law. |
| MAS and agentic AI | MAS specifically recognises autonomous systems accessing tools and plans further sector consultation on useful agentic guidance in **2027**. That future guidance is not yet a final requirement. [MAS announcement](https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/media-releases/2026/mas-sets-out-supervisory-expectations-on-responsible-ai-adoption-by-financial-institutions?trk=public_post_comment-text) | Treat payment execution, credit decisions and customer-record changes as separate capabilities requiring explicit permission boundaries and risk review. |
| Singapore healthcare | MOH/HSA launched **AIHGle 2.0 on 10 March 2026**, addressing generative AI, stakeholder accountability, transparency and deployment risks. [HSA announcement](https://www.hsa.gov.sg/docs/default-source/default-document-library/press-release_imdrf_final.pdf?sfvrsn=f7822396_1) | Separate administrative assistance from clinical recommendations. Add clinical ownership, validation, escalation and change approval. |
| Singapore patient data and medical devices | MOH confirmed on **4 August 2026** that HCSA and PDPA security requirements apply whether patient-data AI runs on-premises or on third-party cloud. HSA separately provides software-medical-device regulatory guidance. [MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/data-security-requirements-apply-to-ai-tools-that-process-patient-data/), [HSA digital health](https://www.hsa.gov.sg/medical-devices/digital-health/) | Require patient-data access controls and vendor review; trigger medical-device classification assessment for diagnostic/treatment functionality. A clinician approval node does not itself settle device classification. |
| EU healthcare | High-risk AI embedded in regulated products has the **2 August 2028** AI Act deadline; sensitive-data processing may already require a GDPR DPIA. [Commission AI timeline](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/ai-omnibus-enters-force), [Commission DPIA guidance](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/obligations_en?prefLang=ro) | Track medical-device requirements, privacy duties and AI Act milestones independently. |
| US healthcare customers | HIPAA permits overseas cloud processing subject to applicable compliance, a business associate agreement and risk analysis; HHS highlights geographic and enforceability risks. [HHS cloud guidance, question 9](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/health-information-technology/cloud-computing/index.html) | Verify BAA coverage for every relevant service in the PHI path. Alibaba Model Studio/agent-service BAA eligibility is **unconfirmed** here; do not claim the solution is HIPAA-ready. |

**Recommended catalog fields and acceptance tests**

These are implementation recommendations derived from the findings above.

| Catalog field / test | Expected behaviour |
|---|---|
| Rule metadata | Store jurisdiction, legal instrument/article, status, applicability conditions, effective date, transition date, source URL and verification date. |
| Role classification | Distinguish model provider, system provider, deployer, controller and processor. Request review when a customer modifies or markets a system under its own name. |
| Routine support-email agent | Add disclosure, privacy/transfer screening, restricted retrieval and tool permissions, audit evidence and escalation. Do not automatically classify all support automation as high-risk. |
| “Rank applicants and reject the lowest-scoring” | Trigger EU high-risk and automated-decision review; Korean high-impact/PIPA review; require a meaningful review and contestability design. |
| “Human approval” implemented as automatic acceptance after timeout | Fail the claimed human-review control; show the unresolved approval path. |
| EU customer, Singapore model | Flag transfer-mechanism and assessment requirements; do not describe Singapore as EU-adequate. |
| Singapore runtime, Hong Kong traces | Show the separate destination and flag personal-data content in telemetry. |
| Generated email or proposal export | Check whether disclosure/provenance survives export; distinguish visible notice from machine-readable marking. |
| Clinical advice or financial execution tools | Require sector classification and controls before recommending autonomous execution. |
| Missing evidence | Display “unconfirmed—customer/vendor verification needed”; never convert an unknown into a compliance badge. |

No China-site-only sources were used. The main unresolved items are inaccessible final Singapore GenAI guidance, some Japanese main-text details, country-specific Southeast Asian transfer conditions, and service-specific vendor contractual eligibility.
